United award fares and booking codes

Last verified: 2026-08-05. Airline inventory rules change without notice; treat anything older than a few months with suspicion.

1. Saver vs. dynamic award pricing

United no longer publishes award charts. On Nov. 15, 2019 it moved awards on its own flights to dynamic pricing, and on April 28, 2020 it deleted the Star Alliance partner award chart from its site — that second change came with no advance notice. Since then there is no official price list for anything; sites like AwardWallet maintain unofficial charts reconstructed from real bookings.

Two kinds of award still exist in practice:

  • Saver awards — capacity-controlled seats filed in dedicated award inventory buckets (the letter codes below). Prices are relatively low and comparatively stable. This is the space worth hunting.
  • Everyday (dynamic) awards — on United-operated flights, any sellable seat can be bought with miles at a dynamically computed price, sometimes absurdly high. These exist even when saver space doesn't.

A concrete example of the split (observed in the seats.aero Partner API response, 2026-08-05): for SFO→NRT on 2026-08-06, a single United availability record reported JAvailable: false with JMileageCost: "0" alongside JAvailableRaw: true with JMileageCostRaw: 360000. The non-Raw fields track saver space; the Raw fields include dynamically priced seats. So that day there was no saver business seat, but you could have a business seat for 360,000 miles at dynamic pricing. "A seat is available" means nothing until you say at what price — which is why this site shows saver space only and deliberately discards the Raw figures.

Why partners price differently: Star Alliance partner programs can generally only see and book saver-level space on each other's flights. A United flight priced dynamically above saver level is bookable with United miles but invisible to Aeroplan, LifeMiles, etc. — and the reverse is true when you point United miles at a Lufthansa or ANA flight. Partner awards booked through United therefore behave close to a fixed (if unpublished) price table, while United-metal awards float.

Sources: TPG: United pulls partner award chart · AwardWallet unofficial MileagePlus chart · The Points Analyst: Star Alliance award availability

2. Cardmember and elite “expanded availability” — and why this site can't see it

Holders of eligible United co-brand cards (including the Club Infinite card) get more than the standard saver space. United's own benefit copy frames it as a discount: eligible primary cardmembers book award flights “on United or United Express only” for “at least 10% fewer miles than the base price paid by members without an eligible card,” rising to “at least 15% fewer miles” for cardmembers who also hold Premier status. These are tiers of one mechanism, not independently stacking pools — the 15% figure already reflects the combined card-plus-elite tier.

Mechanically, it is delivered as extra saver-level inventory in separate booking classes — XN (economy) and IN (business / domestic first) — layered on top of the standard X/I buckets, not as a price cut on the same seat. Sources describe the tiers slightly differently: TPG's current guide assigns XN to cardholders and Premier Silver/Gold, and IN to Premier Platinum, 1K and Global Services, while other coverage describes cardholders unlocking both XN and IN. If IN access matters to you, test it logged in rather than trusting either description.

Two hard limits:

  • United and United Express flights only. Partner-operated space (ANA, Lufthansa, Swiss…) is explicitly excluded — per TPG, “you can't unlock additional award availability when booking through a partner,” and holding the card unlocks nothing extra on partner metal either.
  • Visible only when logged in. XN/IN inventory and the “Special member pricing” it produces appear only when you search united.com signed in to an eligible MileagePlus account. Logged-out searches — and the third-party feeds this site is built on — see only standard X/I saver space.

How to read this site's numbers, then: for United-operated flights the mileage shown here is a ceiling — logged in with the Club Infinite card you may be offered 10–15% less, or see saver space where this site shows none. For partner-operated flights the numbers should match what you see, and partner metal is where most of the good long-haul premium space lives anyway: as of 2026-08-05, every one of the 143 First-class rows in this site's dataset is Air India-operated, and no United-operated row exceeds 140,000 miles.

Two related items, flagged rather than asserted:

  • The Club card family also earns a 10,000-mile award flight discount for every $20,000 of calendar-year spend (max two, at $40,000). United's terms require the award ticket to “have at least one segment booked on United or United Express”; whether the discount can apply to otherwise partner-operated awards beyond that is unconfirmed — we found no clear statement either way.
  • seats.aero has a “Cardholder Pricing” preference (Account → Preferences → United MileagePlus) that surfaces cardholder-discounted results in its own web interface. It does not affect the Partner API, which is what feeds this site. Tested directly on 2026-08-06: with the cardholder and Premier preferences enabled, 643 of 643 comparable award prices returned by the API were byte-identical to those fetched with the preferences off. So enabling it is worth doing for browsing seats.aero itself, but it will never improve the numbers here — the ceiling caveat above still applies in full.

Sources: United: Club card benefits (10%/15% and spend-discount language) · TPG: expanded award availability · AwardWallet: United credit card award space

3. Award booking classes

Award seats live in single-letter inventory buckets (booking classes, or RBDs), separate from the revenue fare classes sold for cash on the same aircraft. Codes ending in N are extra saver space visible only to United elites (Platinum/1K) and United co-brand cardholders.

These are the alliance-standard saver codes. O applies only to partners that still sell international First (ANA, Lufthansa, etc.); United retired its own international First in 2018.

For contrast, United's revenue fare classes on the same plane: Economy Y, B, M, E, U, H, Q, V, W, S, T, L, K, G, N (Basic Economy); Premium Plus O, A, R; Business/First J, C, D, Z, P. Note the overlap: revenue “O” is a paid Premium Plus fare on United but a First-class award on partners — the same letter means different things in different contexts, which is exactly why context matters.

5. Fare basis vs. booking class vs. cabin

Three different things that all get called “fare class” in casual conversation:

TermWhat it isExample
CabinThe physical section of the planePolaris business
Booking class (RBD)Single letter; the inventory bucket your seat was sold or awarded fromI
Fare basisA longer alphanumeric string on the ticket encoding the fare's rules; its first letter is usually the booking classe.g. IJBP0AWD (illustrative)

Worked example: you book a saver business award on a United-operated flight to Frankfurt using partner miles. The cabin is business (Polaris; this site's J). The booking class is I — the saver business award bucket. The ticket carries a fare basis string beginning with I that encodes the award fare's rules. Three labels, one seat.

6. Practical caveats

  • Space is volatile. Saver awards appear and vanish within hours — airlines open and pull buckets continuously, and popular space gets booked fast. Treat any search result as a snapshot, not a promise.
  • seats.aero is a cache, not live inventory. It shows results from periodic background scrapes; the snapshot can be minutes to hours old, and a fresh check may disagree with it. seats.aero's own docs explain that flights can “disappear” simply because a refresh replaced stale cached data.
  • Phantom space exists. Occasionally a partner's systems show award inventory that fails at ticketing (the booking errors out or the seat never confirms). If a too-good award won't ticket, it may never have been real.
  • Visible ≠ bookable with United miles. Partner space surfaced by third-party tools was found through some program's search — not necessarily United's. Programs differ in which partners, routes, and cabins they can ticket, and married-segment and program-specific filters mean United.com may refuse space that Aeroplan happily books (and vice versa). Always confirm on united.com before counting on it.
